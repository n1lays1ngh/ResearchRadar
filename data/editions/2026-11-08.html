Prospective Memory for Proactive LLM Agents
Researchers introduce Pro-Memory, a new framework that gives proactive large language model agents prospective memory to retain future intentions and decide when to act without explicit user requests.
BY YUANCHEN BEI, ZHINING LIU, TIANXIN WEI AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 3 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.05731
THE PROBLEM

Most existing memory systems for large language model agents are retrospective, retrieving past information rather than determining whether a future-directed intention remains pending and when the current situation warrants an action.

THE APPROACH.  The authors propose Pro-Memory, a cognitively inspired framework that separates the maintenance of future intentions from deciding when and how to realize them by assessing new observations against original goals and activation conditions, updating intention states, and utilizing an experience memory for feedback.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Pro-Memory separates maintaining future intentions from deciding when and how to realize them.

■   The framework preserves pending objectives linked to supporting evidence and assesses new observations against original goals and activation conditions.

■   Experiments across two benchmarks—spanning delayed-intention tasks and open-ended proactive assistance—demonstrate consistent improvements in proactive task performance over representative memory approaches.

■   The approach limits unnecessary interventions across multiple large language model backbones.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“For conversational AI, dialogue systems, LLM agents, and retrieval-augmented generation, this shifts agents from passive retrospective recall to active prospective realization, allowing them to provide timely assistance without explicit prompts while controlling disruptive interventions.”

LIMITATIONS.  The study focuses primarily on text-based proactive interactions, while real-world agents may require multimodal intention formation and cross-modal cue recognition, alongside complex long-horizon scenarios involving interdependent intentions and evolving user needs.

Interactive Backdoor Attacks in LLM-Based Proactive Dialogue Systems
Researchers demonstrate that externally supplied dialogue data can be poisoned to implant interactive backdoors in large language model-based proactive dialogue systems, steering multi-turn conversations toward unauthorized attacker objectives.
BY ZHIYANG XIA, YUMING SHANG AND XI ZHANG · SUBMITTED 4 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.06969
THE PROBLEM

Fine-tuning large language model-based proactive dialogue systems on externally supplied dialogue data introduces backdoor risks. Traditional backdoor studies focus primarily on immediate single-turn response deviations upon trigger activation, failing to account for multi-turn conversational guidance in proactive dialogue systems.

THE APPROACH.  The authors present an interactive backdoor attack framework using training-data poisoning to implant a trigger-conditioned guidance-policy bias that ordinary user utterances can activate without deployment-time prompt modification. They instantiate three complementary attacks across progressively expanded interaction settings: Direct Attack, Constrained Progressive Attack, and Unconstrained Progressive Attack.

KEY FINDINGS

■   All three proposed attacks effectively achieve their objectives under their respective interaction settings while largely preserving normal system utility.

■   Experiments were conducted using two large language model backbones and two dialogue datasets within psychological counseling as a representative setting.

■   Tested training-data filters retained nearly all poisoned dialogues.

■   Three post-admission defense baselines left substantial residual attack-specific success.

LIMITATIONS.  In the unconstrained progressive setting, silicon-sampled continuations remain an imperfect proxy that may share structural assumptions with the data-construction process, meaning the findings represent model-side steering within the sampled distribution rather than evidence of real-user susceptibility.

WHY IT MATTERS.  It reveals a critical supply-chain security vulnerability in conversational AI and dialogue systems, demonstrating that data poisoning can manipulate sustained, multi-turn task guidance and conversational steering by LLM agents rather than just isolated single-turn outputs.

Filed under: backdoor attacks · dialogue systems · data poisoning

What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?
New research investigates what multi-turn reinforcement learning actually improves in large language model agents, revealing that its performance gains depend heavily on sufficient interaction budgets rather than uniformly enhancing single-step decisions.
BY KEJIAN ZHU, ZHUORAN JIN, SHANGQING TU AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 4 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.06483
THE PROBLEM

The performance gains of multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning (RL) are highly sensitive to interaction constraints, and it remains poorly understood whether RL uniformly elevates single-step decision quality or specific capabilities like long-horizon resilience.

THE APPROACH.  The authors evaluate agent performance across sampling breadth $K$ and interaction depth $T$ using $Pass@(K,T)$, analyze step-level action quality before and after multi-turn RL, provide a preliminary discussion on process supervision, and introduce BudgetMix RL to vary interaction budgets during training.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Trajectory-level RL advantages depend on sufficient turn budgets and diminish significantly when budgets tighten.

■   Agentic RL does not uniformly elevate single-step decision quality; its strength concentrates in error recovery and long-horizon resilience.

■   RL-generated actions remain locally plausible under the base policy, but complete RL trajectories receive substantially higher likelihood under the RL policy.

■   BudgetMix RL achieves robust performance across diverse execution settings by varying interaction budgets during training.

LIMITATIONS.  The advantages of multi-turn RL diminish, disappear, or even reverse under tight interaction budgets, posing deployment risks in applications with strict constraints.

WHY IT MATTERS.  Understanding how interaction budgets and multi-turn reinforcement learning affect LLM agents ensures that conversational and agentic systems are robust and reliable under realistic deployment constraints rather than evaluated solely on final task success.

Filed under: reinforcement learning · llm agents · interaction budgets

Energy-Efficient Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models
Researchers from NVIDIA have introduced an online controller called EE-RL to reduce GPU energy consumption during reinforcement learning for large language models by dynamically adjusting clock frequencies.
BY RAHUL SHARMA, NITIN VEGESNA AND YANXIN ZHANG · SUBMITTED 3 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.05748
THE PROBLEM

Reinforcement learning for large language models alternates between response generation and policy training, placing different demands on GPUs. Default GPU governors run all phases near peak clock frequency, causing memory-bound work and idle waiting to consume excess power without throughput gains.

THE APPROACH.  The authors present EE-RL (energy-efficient RL), an online controller that selects separate clock frequencies for generation and training during execution. It compares candidate settings with the default GPU governor on the same token workload, keeps the most energy-efficient setting meeting a throughput threshold, and continuously adjusts frequencies as workloads change.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Fixed-frequency sweeps on H100 and GB200 show GPU energy savings of up to 28.2 percent per valid training token across synchronous and asynchronous execution.

■   EE-RL reduces GPU energy per valid training token by up to 16.9 percent at a modest throughput cost.

■   Post-training Olmo 3 32B as a reasoning model used 17 times more datacenter energy than its instruction-tuned counterpart, driven largely by rollout generation.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“The growing electricity demand of AI data centers makes energy efficiency crucial for operating costs and infrastructure expansion. By lowering GPU clock frequencies during phases with different arithmetic intensities, EE-RL reduces energy consumption and electricity costs without substantially extending GPU allocation time.”

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

Tug of Watts: A study on the Energy - Accuracy Trade-off of Large Language Models on Medical Question Answering Tasks
Researchers investigate the energy-accuracy trade-offs of large language models on medical question answering tasks, arguing that disjoint metrics are insufficient for sustainable pipeline design.
BY VIJU SUDHI, GIULIO BILLI, ANDREEA SCROB AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 2 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.02889
THE PROBLEM

While large language models are increasingly deployed in medical question answering systems, studies often overlook their energy consumption and associated carbon footprints in favor of incremental improvements in downstream task accuracy.

THE APPROACH.  The authors examine 13 large language models across 3 model families ranging from 270M to 14B parameters on medical question answering tasks from the OpenMedical-LLM leaderboard. They use the CodeCarbon package to measure inference energy consumption and carbon emissions, and advocate for joint trade-off metrics and Pareto-optimal frontiers.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Larger models attain higher task performance at the cost of higher relative carbon footprints.

■   Reporting disjoint energy and accuracy metrics alone is insufficient for careful trade-off assessments.

■   Systematic construction and interpretation of joint trade-off metrics are required for model evaluation.

■   A distance-based rating scheme can help clearly distinguish the trade-offs of individual models.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  The study addresses the rising inference demands of AI systems—projected to reach 652 TWh by 2030—by helping practitioners treat model selection as a sustainability decision and jointly optimize energy consumption and accuracy.

Energy Measurement and Optimization of TinyML in Resource-Constrained Embedded Systems: A Systematic Mapping Study
A new systematic mapping study examines energy-aware TinyML research, highlighting a heterogeneous landscape lacking standardization in measurement and reporting.
BY CLARIELE ALMEIDA, RENAN BESERRA, RAFAEL MOURA AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 5 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.07264
THE PROBLEM

The literature on TinyML remains heterogeneous regarding evaluated algorithms, energy-measurement methods, reported metrics, influencing factors, and optimization strategies, alongside limited standardization that hinders reproducibility and direct comparison.

THE APPROACH.  The authors present a systematic mapping of energy-aware TinyML research covering studies published between 2020 and 2025, identifying models and learning approaches, classifying energy-evaluation methodologies and metrics, examining technical factors affecting energy consumption, and organizing optimization techniques into a structured taxonomy.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Frequent use of convolutional and lightweight architectures in energy-aware TinyML deployments.

■   Reliance on direct measurements and analytical estimations to evaluate energy consumption.

■   Identification of technical factors related to model architecture, hardware, communication, numerical precision, and input characteristics.

■   Grouping of optimization strategies into model compression, hardware-aware design, system and communication optimization, and data and processing optimization.

LIMITATIONS.  Limited standardization in experimental platforms, measurement procedures, and reporting practices, which constrains reproducibility and direct comparison across studies.

WHY IT MATTERS.  Understanding and organizing energy-measurement methodologies, influencing technical factors, and optimization strategies helps reduce compute overhead, operational costs, and energy usage for resource-constrained edge devices.

FlowBatt: Flow Matching for Probabilistic Battery Degradation Prediction
Researchers have introduced FlowBatt, a conditional generative framework using flow matching and a diffusion transformer backbone to model probabilistic battery degradation trajectories.
BY HAMIDREZA EIVAZI, ESLAM SHARAAWY, STEFAN WITTEK AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 2 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.03150
THE PROBLEM

Battery degradation prediction is difficult due to complex interactions among cycling behavior, operating conditions, and cell chemistry, while existing machine learning approaches largely focus on deterministic end-of-life predictions or fixed chemistry settings rather than probabilistic state of health trajectory modeling.

THE APPROACH.  The authors introduce FlowBatt, a use-inspired conditional generative framework that adapts flow matching with a diffusion transformer (DiT) backbone to model full state of health (SOH) trajectories as probabilistic degradation processes conditioned on early-cycle capacity data. They also use explainable AI to diagnose and improve the encoder that maps early-cycle capacity matrices into conditioning vectors.

KEY FINDINGS

■   FlowBatt achieves the best trajectory-wise SOH prediction performance on all five public benchmark datasets, with errors below 2.5%.

■   The model delivers competitive remaining useful life (RUL) prediction performance compared to established baselines.

■   By generating multiple plausible trajectories, FlowBatt provides empirical prediction intervals for battery degradation modeling.

■   FlowBatt-generated SOH trajectories improve downstream RUL modeling, reducing random-forest RMSE and MAPE on MATR-1 by approximately 26% and 19%, respectively.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“It advances battery management systems by shifting away from deterministic point estimations toward probabilistic, full-trajectory SOH modeling and uncertainty estimation across heterogeneous chemistries.”

LIMITATIONS.  Prediction remains challenging on MATR-2 and HUST, resulting in higher RUL errors and more pronounced undercoverage of empirical prediction intervals. The model conditions only on capacity matrices from fixed 20- or 100-cycle windows without direct temperature, current-profile, charging-protocol, environmental, or cooling inputs, and fixed splits do not establish generalization to unseen chemistries or protocols.

Cross-Domain Joint-Embedding Pretraining For Heterogeneous Industrial Time Series
Researchers have investigated a cross-domain self-supervised pretraining approach called JEPA-HITS to learn reusable time-series representations across physically distinct industrial domains. The shared encoder design improves downstream regression and classification tasks when labeled data are scarce.
BY JONATHAN ADAM RICO, NAGARAJAN RAGHAVAN AND SENTHILNATH JAYAVELU · SUBMITTED 2 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.02489
THE PROBLEM

Industrial predictive maintenance and manufacturing tasks frequently contain abundant sensor measurements, but labeled data for degradation and faults are scarce and expensive. Transfer learning across physically distinct industrial domains remains underexplored because existing models often assume fixed input lengths, specific preprocessing, or rely on closely related datasets.

THE APPROACH.  The authors investigate cross-domain self-supervised pretraining using a joint-embedding predictive architecture for heterogeneous industrial time series (JEPA-HITS). This framework learns a single encoder from diverse domains—such as battery, semiconductor chemical mechanical planarization (CMP), computer numerical control (CNC) machining, and bearing—while preserving dataset-specific temporal scales via multi-domain loss aggregation and a channel-independent latent encoder.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Leave-one-domain-out (LODO) pretraining reduces root mean square error (RMSE) on battery remaining useful life (RUL) regression from 10.72 to 7.95 compared to a randomly initialized architecture.

■   LODO pretraining reduces RMSE from 4.34 to 3.89 on semiconductor chemical mechanical planarization (CMP) and from 13.53 to 11.72 on tool wear regression.

■   LODO pretraining improves bearing fault diagnosis macro-F1 score from 0.547 to 0.737.

■   Unlabeled time series measurements from heterogeneous industrial processes provide reusable representations for downstream learning under reduced-label conditions.

LIMITATIONS.  The work is not evaluated on extreme cases involving signal outliers, missing data, and distribution shifts.

WHY IT MATTERS.  For battery management systems, the ability to leverage unlabeled multi-domain industrial time series data via a shared encoder offers a practical pathway to improve remaining useful life (RUL) and degradation modelling even when battery-specific labeled data are severely limited.

Filed under: battery rul · time series · self-supervised learning
