Forget Freely, Grep Later: Lossless, Index-Free Memory for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
Researchers have developed GrepMem, a memory mechanism for long-horizon LLM agents that uses a simple, lossless plain-text log and regular-expression search to retrieve information without complex indexing.
BY ZIYAN YANG, TAIWEI SHI AND JIEYU ZHAO · SUBMITTED 18 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.25123
THE PROBLEM

Long-horizon LLM agents frequently exceed their context windows, forcing them to discard potentially vital information through lossy compression methods like summarization or masking.

THE APPROACH.  The authors introduce GrepMem, which maintains an append-only plain-text log of the agent's complete history alongside a summarization loop, allowing the agent to perform on-demand recall using a regular-expression search tool.

KEY FINDINGS

■   GrepMem achieves 95-97% accuracy on ContextBench, significantly outperforming lossy baselines which reach at most 21%.

■   The method outperforms SUPO's summarization by 8.8 percentage points on the BCP-Multi benchmark.

■   GrepMem remains on the accuracy-token-cost Pareto frontier across all five evaluation tasks.

■   The system functions effectively without requiring embeddings, retrieval indices, memory extraction, or additional models.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“It offers a lightweight, competitive alternative to complex structured memory systems for LLM agents, demonstrating that preserving raw interaction histories can be more effective than lossy compression for long-horizon tasks.”

LIMITATIONS.  The approach relies on lexical search, which may be ineffective when relevant information cannot be identified through explicit search patterns, and the utility of lossless retrieval depends on the specific task requirements.

AutoRAGBench: A Benchmark for LLM Agents Automating RAG System Development
Researchers introduce AutoRAGBench, a new benchmark designed to evaluate how autonomously large language model agents can build and iteratively refine retrieval-augmented generation systems from scratch. The study demonstrates that advanced agent configurations can successfully automate RAG development, outperforming standard human-designed system baselines.
BY HANG ZHANG, YUZHI PAN, ZIYAO ZHONG AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 19 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.25582
THE PROBLEM

Developing effective retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems requires substantial engineering effort and iterative experimentation because optimal design choices vary significantly across different tasks and domains.

THE APPROACH.  The authors propose AutoRAGBench, a benchmark comprising nine RAG system development tasks. In this framework, evaluated agent configurations build RAG pipelines from scratch in an open code space without starter code or prescribed strategies, operating under a six-hour budget on a single H800 GPU to analyze data, implement code, and refine designs based on experimental feedback.

KEY FINDINGS

■   GPT-6.1 Sol with Codex CLI achieved the highest average score of 56.1 across the benchmark tasks.

■   The composite Human-SOTA baseline achieved an average score of 44.4.

■   When starting from a human-designed system on each of seven tasks, GPT-6.1 Sol with Codex CLI improved the score by 16.6 points on average while preserving the methods' core mechanisms.

■   The evaluation analyzed nine distinct agent configurations across the nine RAG tasks using five answering models.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  For conversational AI and retrieval-augmented generation, automating the complex and time-consuming engineering of RAG pipelines through LLM agents can drastically lower the barrier to building domain-specific, context-grounded assistants. It shifts the development paradigm from manual code tuning to autonomous system design and iterative refinement.

Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB: An Open and Efficient Small Language Model for Fast Conversational Agents
NVIDIA introduces Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB, an open, highly efficient small language model optimized for rapid local conversational agents and multi-turn tool use within a roughly 4 GB memory budget.
BY NVIDIA, :, ADI MARGOLIN AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 16 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.20771
THE PROBLEM

Conversational agents, interactive characters, and robotic interfaces require language models capable of reliable multi-turn tool use, instruction following, and state grounding while responding quickly and fitting within tight memory constraints for local deployment.

THE APPROACH.  The authors further post-trained Gemma-4-E4B-it using reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) via NeMo-RL and NeMo-Gym, optimized both reasoning and non-reasoning modes, added a conversational mode flag for concise spoken dialogue, and paired the model with a DFlash parallel drafter for speculative decoding.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB achieves the highest average accuracy across 21 benchmarks while generating the fewest output tokens in both reasoning and non-reasoning modes compared to Gemma-4-E4B-it and Qwen3.5-4B.

■   When using DFlash, the model achieves 2.74 times the per-user decode speed of inference without speculative decoding at concurrency 1.

■   On the coding subset of SPEED-Bench, the Q4_K_M Bolt E4B with DFlash achieves 1.21 times the generation throughput of Q4_K_M Gemma-4-E4B-it with MTP at batch size 1.

■   The chat template was successfully extended to order text before tool calls and results, allowing the assistant to acknowledge requests or explain actions prior to invocation.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  For conversational AI and dialogue systems, the model demonstrates how small language models can achieve high intelligence and conversational proficiency locally under strict memory limits (~4 GB). For LLM agents, its enhanced multi-turn tool use, instruction following, and DFlash-accelerated decoding provide a blueprint for building fast, responsive agentic workflows on edge devices without relying entirely on heavy cloud infrastructure.

Matching Architecture to Workload: Comparing RISC-V Parallel Accelerators for Energy-Efficient Embedded Computing
Researchers have conducted a controlled comparison of three RISC-V parallel execution models for energy-efficient embedded computing using the same 12 nm technology. They report that no single architecture excels across all workloads, highlighting the need to match execution models to specific computational patterns.
BY LUCA COLOMBO, LUIGI GHIONDA, AMIRHOSSEIN KIAMARZI AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 19 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.25947
THE PROBLEM

Embedded and edge computing systems face strict power budgets, silicon footprint limits, and memory bandwidth constraints while running diverse workloads with distinct computational characteristics—such as regular linear algebra, digital signal processing, machine learning, and irregular control-heavy tasks—making it difficult to choose a single optimal execution model.

THE APPROACH.  The authors compare three execution models for energy-efficient embedded computing: scalar multicore, streaming multicore, and vector processing. They evaluate three open-source RISC-V accelerators as representative implementations—the PULP Cluster, the Snitch Cluster, and Spatz, respectively—using implementations in the same 12 nm technology and a common benchmark suite.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Spatz achieves up to 4.29x higher performance and 5.17x greater energy efficiency than the other architectures on regular and highly vectorizable compute-intensive applications.

■   The Snitch Cluster provides up to 5.53x higher performance and 1.45x greater energy efficiency on applications with limited parallelism and streamable memory-access patterns.

■   The PULP Cluster excels on irregular and control-dominated applications, yielding improvements of up to 5.66x in performance and 2.80x in energy efficiency.

■   The greater flexibility offered by the PULP and Snitch clusters incurs a non-negligible cost in terms of both area and power compared to Spatz.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“Edge deployment of machine learning and language models requires high energy efficiency under tight power and carbon constraints. Understanding how different workload classes map to specific hardware architectures provides practical guidance for designing heterogeneous systems that minimize compute overhead and power consumption.”

LIMITATIONS.  Spatz exhibits the greatest performance variability and is highly sensitive to workload characteristics, with its worst-case results often falling below those of the other two architectures.

Splice-Core: Low-Order-Bit-Splicing GEMM for Highly Energy-Efficient AI Inference Accelerators
Researchers have introduced Splice-Core, a hybrid matrix multiplication unit that uses low-order bit-splicing to significantly improve energy efficiency and throughput in AI inference accelerators.
BY PRABHU VELLAISAMY, HSIEN-FU HUNG, ANDREW KIM AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 19 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.25617
THE PROBLEM

Temporal-unary design efficiency varies with precision, array size, and operand distribution, while low-order accumulation latency can degrade performance in AI inference workloads.

THE APPROACH.  The authors developed Splice-Core, a temporal-unary/binary hybrid general matrix multiplication (GEMM) unit featuring two processing element variants: Splice-BP (utilizing bit planes) and Splice-LUT (selecting a shared weight multiple in a single cycle).

KEY FINDINGS

■   Splice-BP and Splice-LUT achieve 77.4% and 76.4% geometric-mean tile energy reductions, respectively, compared to a binary systolic array across 26 INT4 CNN and LLM workloads in 45nm.

■   Splice-LUT increases geometric-mean tile throughput by 44.4% and reduces geometric-mean energy-delay product by 6.13x across the 26 configurations.

■   Across evaluated W8A8 convolution and expert-projection workloads, Splice-BP and Splice-LUT reduce tile energy by 77.3% to 88.1%.

■   Post-place-and-route INT4 evaluation yields 14% and 9% smaller areas for Splice-BP and Splice-LUT, respectively, compared to the systolic array.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  By drastically cutting energy consumption and chip area for INT4 and INT8 AI inference workloads without sacrificing accuracy, Splice-Core enables more sustainable machine learning hardware with lower compute costs and energy footprints.

Filed under: ai accelerators · energy efficiency · gemm

How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods
A new doctoral thesis explores advanced energy management methods for residential systems, introducing a machine learning approach that balances competitive performance with interpretability.
BY JULIAN RUDDICK · SUBMITTED 19 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.26412
THE PROBLEM

The transition from fossil fuels creates energy management challenges, requiring methods to reduce energy consumption and synchronize electricity usage and storage with low-emission sources.

THE APPROACH.  The author compares simple rules, optimization, and machine learning methods across three simulation cases and one real-world experiment, and introduces TreeC, a novel machine learning-based method designed to learn understandable and efficient energy management strategies.

KEY FINDINGS

■   The developed TreeC method demonstrates competitive performance compared to approaches like model predictive control and reinforcement learning across diverse energy management problems.

■   TreeC's understandability improves the transparency and trustworthiness of machine learning-based energy management.

■   A real-world experiment revealed that TreeC and model predictive control performed worse than in simulations due to insufficiently representative training data and unreliable real-time input data, respectively.

■   Energy management algorithms reduce costs in electrified Belgian dwellings—particularly poorly insulated homes, electric vehicle chargers, and photovoltaic installations—though average savings do not yet outweigh current implementation costs and efforts.

LIMITATIONS.  Real-world performance is hindered by insufficiently representative training data for machine learning approaches and unreliable real-time input data for model predictive control, and current cost savings do not yet outweigh implementation costs and effort.

WHY IT MATTERS.  The research addresses how machine learning techniques can be made more transparent and trustworthy for managing residential energy systems, balancing computational strategies with practical cost and deployment realities.

MARS: Feature-guided physics-informed deep learning for real-time battery health and lifetime prediction
Researchers have proposed a new multi-scale adaptive robust framework called MARS to improve battery health and remaining useful life predictions under variable operating conditions. The method combines a dual-branch temporal encoder with a differentiable generalized-correntropy adaptive Kalman filter.
BY INNOCENT IZABAYO AND HAIQUAN ZHAO · SUBMITTED 17 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.22455
THE PROBLEM

Accurate estimation of battery state-of-health and remaining useful life remains challenging in electrified transportation systems due to operating variability, non-stationary aging, and non-Gaussian measurement disturbances.

THE APPROACH.  The authors propose Multi-scale Adaptive Robust State Estimation (MARS), a framework that couples a dual-branch temporal encoder with an equivalent-circuit-model-constrained differentiable generalized-correntropy adaptive Kalman filter. The encoder separates fast intra-cycle dynamics from slow inter-cycle degradation, while uncertainty decomposition and Monte Carlo dropout generate calibrated prediction intervals.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Experiments on the NASA benchmark demonstrate state-of-health mean absolute error and root mean square error values of 0.72% and 0.92%, respectively.

■   Experiments on the CALCE benchmark demonstrate state-of-health mean absolute error and root mean square error values of 0.63% and 0.80%, respectively.

■   Conditional remaining useful life prediction intervals achieve coverage probabilities of 100.00% on NASA and 99.24% on CALCE.

■   Computational profiling on a laptop graphics processing unit indicates inference times of a few milliseconds per cycle.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“Accurate state-of-health estimation and remaining useful life prediction are crucial enabling functions for battery management systems, supporting preventive maintenance, warranty-risk management, fleet-level planning, and safe, cost-effective operation of electrified transportation systems.”

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

Accessing Electrode Edges: Upright Mounting for Site-Specific Cryogenic Atom Probe Tomography of Lithium-Ion Batteries
Researchers have developed an upright-mounting workflow for cryogenic atom probe tomography that enables targeted site-specific analysis of edge-localised features in lithium-ion battery electrodes.
BY SYEDA RAMIN JANNAT, JAMES O. DOUGLAS, NEIL MULCAHY AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 16 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.21357
THE PROBLEM

Site-specific preparation of localised features and liquid-solid interfaces remains challenging in battery systems, particularly when regions of interest are concentrated along electrode edges that are difficult to access using conventional flat mounting geometries.

THE APPROACH.  The authors present a cryo-APT workflow incorporating an upright-mounting procedure, where folded copper tape secures the electrode perpendicular to a conventional cryogenic sample holder, allowing targeted specimen preparation using plasma focused ion beam microscopy and milling under cryogenic conditions.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Successfully prepared and analysed an electrolyte-wetted graphite electrode specimen containing an edge-localised electrolyte deposit alongside underlying graphite and copper current collector.

■   Identified electrolyte-associated species including Li, HxF, P, and CxHyOz in the wetted electrode specimen.

■   Successfully applied the workflow to a cycled graphite electrode to target and analyse lithium dendrites concentrated around the perimeter.

■   Allowed different phases to be positioned sequentially along the atom probe tomography field-evaporation axis rather than laterally.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  Accurate characterisation of degradation phenomena such as lithium plating, dendrite formation, and interface evolution along electrode edges is vital for understanding battery degradation mechanisms, which directly underpins reliable state-of-charge, state-of-health estimation, and remaining useful life prediction models.

Weakly coordinating anions and solvents break the conductivity-transference-solvation tradeoff
Lithium-metal batteries promise high energy density, but common electrolytes trade ionic conductivity against the share of current carried by lithium ions and against how strongly solvent binds those ions.
BY ALEX LIU, GANESH RAGHAVENDRAN, ERIC S. YANG AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 19 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.25375

THE PROBLEM.  Not stated in the provided text.

THE APPROACH.  We show that simultaneously weakening both interactions of the lithium ion, with solvent and with anion, relaxes this trade-off. Thus, a weakly coordinating perfluorinated alkoxyaluminate salt in liquefied fluoromethane conducts 39.2 mS cm$^{-1}$ at 25 $^\circ$C while maintaining a lithium-ion transference number of 0.71.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Consistent with these properties, nickel-rich cathode/lithium-metal anode full cells charge at 10C at room temperature and at 2C at $-40$ $^\circ$C.

■   We advance coordination strength as a design axis for future energy storage devices.
