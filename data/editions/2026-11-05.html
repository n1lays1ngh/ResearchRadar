Prospective Memory for Proactive LLM Agents
The paper introduces Pro-Memory, a cognitively inspired memory framework that equips proactive LLM agents with prospective memory to retain future intentions and realize them at the right time.
BY YUANCHEN BEI, ZHINING LIU, TIANXIN WEI AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 3 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.05731
THE PROBLEM

Most existing LLM agent memory systems are retrospective—they retrieve past information to inform current decisions—which fails to address the needs of proactive agents that must remember future-directed intentions and decide when to act without explicit user requests.

THE APPROACH.  The authors propose Pro-Memory, a framework that separates the maintenance of future intentions from deciding when and how to realize them. It preserves pending objectives with supporting evidence, assesses new observations against activation conditions, updates intention states based on actions or resolution, and uses an experience memory to distill feedback from completed episodes.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Experiments across two benchmarks (delayed-intention tasks and open-ended proactive assistance) show consistent improvements in proactive task performance over representative memory approaches.

■   The framework operates successfully across multiple LLM backbones.

■   It limits unnecessary interventions while timing actions appropriately.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“For LLM agents and conversational dialogue systems, transitioning from passive retrospective retrieval to active prospective memory is crucial for enabling proactive assistance, allowing agents to initiate helpful actions at the correct moment without causing disruptive or premature interventions.”

LIMITATIONS.  The current study focuses primarily on text-based proactive interactions rather than multimodal signals (like images, audio, and video), and does not yet address complex long-horizon scenarios with interdependent intentions or real-world safety guarantees.

REPORT · CS.LG · MACHINE LEARNING
What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents?
Multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning improves long-horizon LLM agent performance, but these gains heavily depend on having sufficient interaction turn budgets.
BY KEJIAN ZHU, ZHUORAN JIN, SHANGQING TU AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 4 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.06483
THE PROBLEM

It is poorly understood what capabilities multi-turn agentic reinforcement learning (RL) actually improves in LLM agents, and how reliable these agents are under realistic interaction constraints.

THE APPROACH.  The authors evaluate agentic RL using Pass@(K,T) metrics across sampling breadth (K) and interaction depth (T), analyze step-level action quality, and introduce BudgetMix RL to vary interaction budgets during training.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Trajectory-level RL advantages depend on sufficient turn budgets and diminish significantly when budgets tighten.

■   Agentic RL does not uniformly elevate single-step decision quality; its strength concentrates in error recovery and long-horizon resilience.

■   BudgetMix RL achieves robust performance across diverse execution settings by varying interaction budgets during training.

LIMITATIONS.  The gains of agentic RL shrink under tight interaction budgets, and single-step action quality does not consistently improve, presenting deployment risks in applications with strict budgets or irreversible actions.

WHY IT MATTERS.  It demonstrates that LLM agents and conversational systems evaluated solely on final task success may fail under tight interaction constraints, highlighting the need to evaluate robustness across execution constraints and individual decision reliability.

REPORT · CS.AI · ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
WASP-Mem: Let Agent Organize Procedural Memory. Human Spend Effort on Diagnosis
The paper introduces WASP-Mem, a training-free method that consolidates execution traces and feedback into persistent wiki pages to improve LLM agent procedural memory.
BY PEIZHI NIU, GE SHI, VAIBHAV GORDE AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 2 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.04003
THE PROBLEM

Large language model (LLM) agents struggle to automate complex enterprise procedures because static harnesses cannot capture proprietary policies, undocumented behaviors, or state-dependent recovery paths that only appear during execution.

THE APPROACH.  The authors propose WASP-Mem (Wiki-based Agent-Synthesized Procedural Memory), which distills execution traces and feedback into persistent, interlinked wiki pages to enable continual agent learning without changing model parameters.

KEY FINDINGS

■   WASP-Mem raises the checkpoint pass rate by 4.6-6.1% over the strongest baseline across three models while requiring 57-61% less storage.

■   Prescriptive knowledge organization, such as human-defined schemas and explicit deduplication rules, hurts performance.

■   Human diagnosis converts failed traces into corrective knowledge and drives sustained improvement.

■   A single round of human diagnosis outperforms four rounds of self-judgment by 7.0-10.9%.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  It demonstrates an effective, training-free method for long-term procedural memory management in LLM agents, outlining a clear division of labor where agents organize memory and humans focus selectively on failure diagnosis.

Filed under: llm agents · procedural memory · continual learning

Energy-Efficient Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models
EE-RL is an online controller that dynamically selects separate GPU clock frequencies for the generation and training phases of large language model reinforcement learning, reducing energy consumption while preserving throughput.
BY RAHUL SHARMA, NITIN VEGESNA AND YANXIN ZHANG · SUBMITTED 3 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.05748
THE PROBLEM

Reinforcement learning for large language models alternates between generation and training phases that have different hardware demands. The default GPU governor runs everything near peak clock frequency, causing memory-bound and waiting phases to consume excessive power without proportional throughput gains, while manual frequency tuning is too expensive and complex.

THE APPROACH.  The authors introduce EE-RL (energy-efficient RL), an online controller that measures energy per valid training token (response tokens consumed by training that contribute to the loss) and throughput. It tests candidate frequency settings against the default GPU governor in short trials, selects the most energy-efficient setting within a throughput limit, and continuously adjusts frequencies as the workload changes.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Fixed-frequency sweeps on H100 and GB200 show GPU energy savings of up to 28.2% per valid training token across synchronous and asynchronous execution while largely preserving throughput.

■   EE-RL turns this hardware headroom into online savings, reducing GPU energy per valid training token by up to 16.9% at a modest throughput cost.

■   Different RL phases (autoregressive generation vs. parallel training batch processing) expose distinct arithmetic intensities and hardware demands.

■   Post-training an Olmo 3 32B model used 17x more datacenter energy than its instruction-tuned counterpart, highlighting the need for phase-aware energy optimization.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“By optimizing GPU clock frequencies dynamically during reinforcement learning post-training, this approach lowers electricity costs, reduces datacenter carbon footprints, and improves the sustainability of heavy AI training workloads without requiring tedious manual pre-tuning.”

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

Tug of Watts: A study on the Energy - Accuracy Trade-off of Large Language Models on Medical Question Answering Tasks

THE PROBLEM

While recent machine learning research focuses on incrementally improving downstream task accuracy in healthcare applications like medical question answering (QA), the associated energy consumption and carbon footprints during inference are frequently overlooked.

THE APPROACH.  The authors examine 13 LLMs from 3 model families ranging from 270M to 14B parameters on medical QA tasks from the OpenMedical-LLM leaderboard. They use the CodeCarbon package to measure inference energy consumption and carbon emissions, and evaluate the models using joint trade-off metrics, Pareto-optimal frontiers, and a distance-based rating scheme.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Larger models achieve higher task performance but at the cost of significantly higher relative carbon footprints.

■   Reporting disjoint energy and accuracy metrics alone is insufficient for careful trade-off assessments; systematic joint metrics are required.

■   Utilized Pareto-optimal frontiers and a distance-based rating scheme to clearly distinguish the trade-offs of individual models.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  It addresses the soaring energy demands of real-time AI inference (projected to reach 652 TWh by 2030) by providing practitioners with methods to jointly optimize energy consumption and accuracy, helping them choose more efficient models and build sustainable machine learning pipelines.

LEAD STORY · CS.LG · MACHINE LEARNING
FlowBatt: Flow Matching for Probabilistic Battery Degradation Prediction
FlowBatt is a conditional generative framework that uses flow matching and a diffusion transformer to predict probabilistic battery state-of-health (SOH) trajectories.
BY HAMIDREZA EIVAZI, ESLAM SHARAAWY, STEFAN WITTEK AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 2 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.03150
THE PROBLEM

Existing machine learning methods for battery health primarily focus on deterministic point predictions for remaining useful life (RUL) or SOH, failing to capture the probabilistic nature of degradation trajectories across diverse chemistries.

THE APPROACH.  The authors introduce FlowBatt, which uses a diffusion transformer (DiT) backbone to model full SOH degradation trajectories conditioned on early-cycle capacity data, while using explainable AI to optimize the input encoder.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Achieved the best trajectory-wise SOH prediction performance across five public benchmark datasets.

■   Maintained prediction errors below 2.5% for SOH trajectories.

■   Delivered competitive RUL prediction performance compared to specialized baselines.

■   Provided empirical prediction intervals by generating multiple plausible degradation trajectories.

■   Reduced random forest RMSE and MAPE on the MATR-1 dataset by approximately 26% and 19% respectively using synthetic trajectories.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“It shifts battery management from simple point-based RUL estimation to probabilistic trajectory modeling, allowing for better uncertainty quantification and more robust health forecasting across heterogeneous battery chemistries.”

LIMITATIONS.  The model lacks direct inputs for environmental factors like temperature or charging protocols, does not enforce physical constraints, and shows higher RUL errors and undercoverage of prediction intervals on specific datasets (MATR-2 and HUST).

Battery energy storage system (BESS) flexibility depends on state of charge and intertemporal operating constraints.

THE PROBLEM.  Not stated in the provided text.

THE APPROACH.  Renewable forecast errors of similar magnitudes can lead to different BESS dispatch results. An error may alter early charging or discharging decisions and reduce the flexibility available for subsequent regulation, while another may have little effect on the resulting schedule.

KEY FINDINGS

■   To account for these unequal consequences, this paper proposes Frequency-Calibrated Decision-Focused Learning (FC-DFL), which trains renewable predictors using the realized operating costs of their induced BESS schedules.

Cross-Domain Joint-Embedding Pretraining For Heterogeneous Industrial Time Series
Industrial predictive maintenance and manufacturing tasks frequently contain abundant sensor measurements, but with limited labeled data.
BY JONATHAN ADAM RICO, NAGARAJAN RAGHAVAN AND SENTHILNATH JAYAVELU · SUBMITTED 2 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.02489

THE PROBLEM.  Not stated in the provided text.

THE APPROACH.  We investigate cross-domain self-supervised pretraining using joint-embedding predictive architecture for heterogeneous industrial time series (JEPA-HITS), which learns one encoder from various domains such as battery, semiconductor chemical mechanical planarization (CMP), computer numerical control (CNC) machining, and bearing while preserving dataset-specific temporal scales. We evaluate representation reuse on regression and classification problems under reduced-label downstream training, and leave-one-domain-out (LODO) pretraining.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Overall results suggest that unlabeled time series measurements from heterogeneous industrial processes can provide reusable representations for downstream learning with limited labeled data.
