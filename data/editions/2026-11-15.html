BioMem: Agentic Memory for Biomedical LLM Agents
Researchers systematically investigate agentic memory systems in the biomedical domain and introduce a training-free framework called HMRU to enhance evidence accessibility and task-aware utilization.
BY MINGCHEN LI, YONGKANG XIAO, RUMENG LI AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 10 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.13426
THE PROBLEM

The paper tackles the fundamental question of how effectively and reliably agentic memory systems retain, retrieve, update, and utilize biomedical information across tasks and interactions.

THE APPROACH.  The authors systematically investigate agentic memory across four dimensions (task effectiveness, retrieval quality, information fidelity, and long-term robustness) by evaluating five memory approaches across four large language models in three settings: biomedical knowledge reasoning, clinical prediction, and long-term conversational memory. They also introduce Hybrid Memory Retrieval and Utilization (HMRU), a training-free framework.

KEY FINDINGS

■   HMRU achieves an average relative improvement of 8.6% across biomedical question-answering and conversational memory benchmarks, with additional gains in selected clinical prediction tasks.

■   HMRU reduces conversational answer errors by 24.9% and closes 48.0% of the performance gap to full-context inference.

■   Retrieving relevant information does not guarantee correct utilization, and semantic similarity does not necessarily correspond to clinical outcome similarity.

■   Iterative memory updates may introduce source-unsupported content, and long-term conversational memory performance cannot be explained by history length alone.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“It demonstrates how persistent memory systems, evidence retrieval, and dynamic information updating can be optimized in conversational AI and LLM agents to improve reliability and performance in complex domains such as biomedical applications.”

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

ToolTurns: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Multi-Turn Dialogue
Researchers introduce ToolTurns, a benchmark for evaluating tool-using large language models across multi-turn dialogues with broad API coverage, revealing that agents struggle to sustain success over extended conversations.
BY MILAD GHOLAMI, BRENDAN KING AND JEFFREY FLANIGAN · SUBMITTED 11 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.14925
THE PROBLEM

Most large-scale tool-use benchmarks focus on isolated requests rather than evaluating tool-using language models across conversational settings that require clarification, tool use, and retention of conversational context over multi-turn dialogues.

THE APPROACH.  The authors introduce ToolTurns, a benchmark consisting of 2,620 problems spanning 1,827 tools and 11,004 API endpoints across 47 categories, featuring an intentionally underspecified initial request followed by two context-dependent follow-ups. They pair this with an open-source user simulator featuring a checker-fixer pipeline and evaluate nine language models.

KEY FINDINGS

■   The best-performing agent resolves 69.6% of individual requests but completes only 39.8% of full dialogues.

■   Every evaluated model receives lower success scores on follow-up requests than on the initial request.

■   Dialogue-quality rubric scores and task success can rank models very differently.

■   A checker-fixer pipeline reduces detected dialogue defects by 41.2% among rewritten messages, and blind human evaluation favors the revised turns in 63.3% of sampled pairs.

LIMITATIONS.  Measured difficulty mixes the agent with the simulated user's choices, tool observations come from a cache or LLM simulation rather than live calls, initial queries and withheld context are generated rather than written by humans, every dialogue is scored on a fixed three-request horizon, and evaluation relies on LLM judges.

WHY IT MATTERS.  It establishes that evaluating conversational AI and LLM agents requires tracking end-to-end task completion throughout multi-turn interactions rather than relying solely on isolated request metrics or dialogue-quality rubrics.

Unprompted coordination through shared memory in LLM agents: a locked two-implementation replication and extension
Researchers report that large language-model agents can unprompted coordinate through shared memory infrastructure without knowing other agents exist. The behaviour is governed by specific intermediate writing steps and minor workspace instructions.
BY PEKKA ABRAHAMSSON, MUHAMMAD WASEEM, ESA KARJALAINEN AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 9 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.12412
THE PROBLEM

Large language-model agents are frequently deployed in groups that share underlying infrastructure such as caches and memory services. It remains rarely examined whether these agents can coordinate through shared state alone without any explicit instruction or disclosure that other agents are present.

THE APPROACH.  The authors evaluated four agents in a minimal environment receiving short arithmetic tasks and a key-value store presented as an individual scratch space, with no prompt mentioning collaboration. The study utilized an independently rebuilt environment from a written protocol, operating under hashed locks on design, data, and analysis across multiple models and two implementations.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Joint success between agents occurred exclusively through the shared store acting as an unprompted communication channel.

■   Whether a model coordinated depended on whether it wrote an intermediate result and looked at the store again, rather than its vendor tier.

■   A single sentence in the prompt, 'check your workspace again before submitting', switched the second look on and off.

■   The qualitative patterns held under an independent, locked replication, though implementation details like strict parsers and generic key names influenced outcomes.

LIMITATIONS.  Future work needs to test open-weight models and separate using another agent's output from the explicit intention to communicate.

WHY IT MATTERS.  As conversational AI and LLM agents are deployed at scale with shared caches and memory services, unintended shared-state communication can emerge as a security risk and an uncontrolled side channel, requiring separate and logged namespaces.

Sustainable Federated Fine-Tuning with LoRA: Environmental Impact and Performance Footprint
Researchers have evaluated the energy and carbon costs of federated fine-tuning for Large Language Models using Low-Rank Adaptation, finding that local optimization steps heavily dictate energy consumption.
BY PEDRO AUGUSTO LONDE RIBEIRO, RODRIGO MOREIRA, LARISSA FERREIRA RODRIGUES MOREIRA AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 12 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.16856
THE PROBLEM

The energy and carbon costs of Federated Learning combined with Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning across diverse hardware remain poorly understood.

THE APPROACH.  The authors evaluated federated Low-Rank Adaptation fine-tuning on NVIDIA A40, RTX 6000 Ada, and A100 GPUs using a $2^2$ full factorial design with 135M/360M models and 10/100 local steps, tracking energy and estimated carbon emissions using CodeCarbon.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Descriptive allocation of variation assigned 84.8-91.2% of energy and emissions variation to local steps within the tested ranges.

■   On NVIDIA A40 and RTX 6000 Ada GPUs, configurations with fewer local steps achieved comparable accuracy with roughly 6-8-fold lower energy consumption.

■   Configurations with fewer local steps yielded higher accuracy per kWh.

■   Local optimization intensity outweighed model size in energy use and CO2-equivalent emissions within the tested parameters.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“It demonstrates that smaller local compute budgets in federated environments can drastically reduce energy consumption and carbon emissions during Large Language Model adaptation without sacrificing predictive accuracy.”

LIMITATIONS.  The evaluation covers one model family, dataset, and seed, and platform comparisons depend on accounting boundaries and predictive quality.

REPORT · CS.AI · ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
Toward Sustainable Agentic AI: Carbon Tradeoffs Across Iterative Self-Refinement and Tool-Augmented Coding
Researchers have quantified the environmental costs of agentic AI, revealing that iterative self-refinement and tool-augmented coding significantly increase carbon footprints compared to single-pass model usage.
BY BORIS RUF, ADRIEN DE MAILLY NESLE AND MARCIN DETYNIECKI · SUBMITTED 9 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.12128
THE PROBLEM

The environmental impact of agentic AI systems, which rely on iterative self-refinement or external tool usage to improve performance, remains largely underexplored despite their increasing adoption.

THE APPROACH.  The authors conducted two empirical studies: Study 1 evaluated the carbon footprint of GPT-4.1-mini using iterative verbal self-refinement on MATH benchmark tasks, while Study 2 analyzed the carbon and cost efficiency of three models (GPT-4.1-mini, Claude Opus 4.6, and GPT-5.3-codex) on the SWE-bench Lite coding benchmark.

KEY FINDINGS

■   In Study 1, three refinement steps closed a 16-percentage-point accuracy gap but nearly tripled the carbon footprint compared to a single-pass baseline.

■   In Study 2, GPT-5.3-codex achieved the highest task resolution rate (81.2%) but incurred approximately 3.3 times higher estimated carbon per resolved task than GPT-4.1-mini.

■   The research demonstrates that capability gains in agentic AI do not necessarily align with sustainability goals.

■   Carbon estimates provided are lower bounds, as they exclude prompt tokens—which account for over 99% of tokens in Study 2—and tool execution costs.

LIMITATIONS.  The current carbon estimates are restricted to output-token inference only, excluding the significant energy costs associated with prompt tokens and tool execution.

WHY IT MATTERS.  As agentic systems become more prevalent, understanding the trade-offs between model performance and environmental cost is essential for developing sustainable AI deployment strategies that minimize compute and carbon usage.

Scaling Shapley Energy Attribution to Large LLM Batches
Researchers introduce StepSplit, a method to efficiently calculate fair energy attribution for large batches of large language model requests without requiring infeasible numbers of GPU runs.
BY QI LUO, ZIWEN WANG, KUNLIN LI AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 10 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.13802
THE PROBLEM

Serving engines batch multiple LLM requests together and GPUs report only aggregate energy consumption. Traditional methods estimate an individual request's energy share in proportion to its token consumption, which produces substantially unfair estimates, while computing the theoretically sound Shapley value directly requires $2^N-1$ GPU runs and is infeasible for large batches.

THE APPROACH.  The authors propose StepSplit, observing that inference step energy consists of a shared component (largely from reading model weights) and a request-specific component. This allows them to derive a closed-form Shapley value computed via a one-time microbenchmark and a single pass over scheduler logs under static batching, or with an added CPU-computed correction under continuous batching.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Under static batching with batch sizes of 8-256, StepSplit achieves an L1 error of 0.4-4.3%, compared to 13.4-61.3% for token-proportional allocation.

■   Under continuous batching with 8-128 requests, StepSplit achieves an L1 error of 0.5-6.4%, compared to 20.6-71.7% for token-proportional allocation.

■   A one-time microbenchmark lasting 2.5-17.8 minutes calibrates the step energy for each model and GPU configuration.

■   Reading model weights accounts for 11.0-89.8% of the inference pass energy depending on the workload.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

WHY IT MATTERS.  Accurate and fair energy attribution is critical for assessing the true environmental impact, carbon footprint, and financial cost of serving individual LLM requests, enabling better scheduling and billing in sustainable machine learning infrastructure.

LEAD STORY · COND-MAT.MTRL-SCI · MATERIALS SCIENCE
Lithium redistribution and electrode degradation in commercial NMC/Graphite and LCO/Graphite cells under high-rate cycling conditions
Researchers investigate how different cathode chemistries and high-rate cycling conditions accelerate degradation in commercial lithium-ion cells, identifying a sharp transition in stability between 1C and 2C.
BY NAISARGI KANABAR, NICHOLAS FUSCO, SEIICHIRO HIGASHIYA AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 11 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.15818
THE PROBLEM

Fast charging accelerates degradation in lithium-ion batteries, but the specific effects of cathode chemistry and charging protocols remain difficult to separate.

THE APPROACH.  Commercial NMC811/graphite and LiCoO2/graphite coin cells were cycled for 100 cycles at 0.3C, 1C, 2C, and 3C, and subsequently analysed using scanning electron microscopy, X-ray diffraction, and nuclear reaction analysis.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Both cell types exhibited a transition in cycling stability between 1C and 2C.

■   After 100 cycles, NMC811/graphite retained 72.3% and 63.5% of its capacity at 2C and 3C respectively, while LiCoO2/graphite retained only 11.1% at 2C.

■   Nuclear reaction analysis revealed maximum lithium accumulation in graphite anodes at 2C for both chemistries, accompanied by granular surface deposits.

■   The largest absolute capacity loss occurred for NMC811/graphite at 2C, amounting to 44.99 mAh.

■   The LiCoO2/graphite 3C condition was limited by its charging protocol rather than intrinsic degradation alone.

 
WHY IT MATTERS
“Understanding rate-dependent degradation pathways, anode-side lithium accumulation, and protocol limitations is critical for improving state-of-health estimation, degradation modelling, and remaining useful life prediction in battery management systems.”

LIMITATIONS.  The influence of experimental conditions cannot be fully excluded, and future studies using identical constant-current and constant-current-constant-voltage protocols are required.

Degradation-Aware Participation of Battery Storage in Frequency Regulation Markets
As data centers make the load on the power grid fluctuate faster, frequency regulation grows more valuable.
BY ANAND GOKHALE AND SIDDHARTH BHELA · SUBMITTED 9 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.12555

THE PROBLEM.  Not stated in the provided text.

THE APPROACH.  Battery energy storage systems (BESSs) are well suited to provide frequency regulation, but providing this service speeds up aging, especially during operation at extreme state of charge (SoC). In the PJM interconnection, the grid operator supplies a regulation signal that a participating BESS is expected to follow, within a capacity that it must bid before the signal is known.

KEY FINDINGS

■   Accordingly, we decompose the degradation-aware frequency regulation problem into a bidding layer and an operations layer.

■   For bidding, we propose an adaptive strategy, assuming perfect tracking in which the bid depends on the SoC expected at the start of the bid period by means of choosing basepoints.

■   For operation, the BESS further protects its battery by deliberately giving up a perfect performance score, to keep its SoC away from the extremes.

LIMITATIONS.  Not stated in the provided text.

Robust State-Parameter Estimation in Second-Order Thévenin Batteries
We propose a scheme for estimating the full states and parameters of a second-order Thévenin battery model, using distinct features of the measured current.
BY WISSAM KHENNOUNE, G. Q. BAO TRAN, THACH NGOC DINH AND OTHERS · SUBMITTED 9 NOVEMBER 2026 · ARXIV:2611.13202

THE PROBLEM.  Not stated in the provided text.

THE APPROACH.  An identification step, run during suitable rest intervals, identifies the resistances and capacitances of the two RC branches from the voltage relaxation by linear least squares, without knowledge of the open-circuit voltage (OCV) map. An online estimator then runs continuously: the polarization voltages are estimated by open-loop observers; the series resistance is updated at current jumps using least squares; the state of charge (SOC) is propagated by Coulomb counting using the current capacity estimate, and both estimates are updated periodically by a sliding-window regression.

KEY FINDINGS

■   We show that the identification is exact under a mild rank condition, that the series resistance is recovered at the first current jump, and that the online state and capacity estimates converge exponentially under verifiable excitation conditions.

■   We also establish robustness of the online estimator under bounded process disturbances and measurement noise.
