ARXIV CS.CL
Computation and Language

Recent research addresses core challenges in conversational artificial intelligence and language model agents. The studies introduce new benchmarks and frameworks to improve multi-turn tool use, agent memory, speech preferences, spoken dialogue adaptation, and reinforcement learning.

1
ToolTurns: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Multi-Turn Dialogue
The authors introduce ToolTurns, a benchmark of 2,620 problems to evaluate large language model agents in multi-turn dialogues, finding that models perform worse on follow-up requests than on initial ones.
Milad Gholami, Brendan King and others · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Human-Likeness and Conversational Preference Diverge for Synthesized Agent Speech in Customer-Service Calls
The authors examine customer-service agent speech and find that human-likeness and conversational preference diverge, meaning systems can rank differently on the two judgments.
Jeffrey M. Girard, Antony D'Avirro and others · cs.CL, cs.HC, eess.AS  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
We Are What We Remember: Mental-State-Driven Memory for Long-Term Character Dialogue
The authors propose MindMem, a mental-state-driven memory framework that consolidates shared experiences into persistent states, and evaluate it using a new longitudinal benchmark called MemReal-Bench.
Zhicheng Jia, Shuchen Wu and others · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
Outcome-Mass Alignment: Controlling Length-Dependent Trajectory Influence in Multi-Turn LLM Agent Reinforcement Learning
The authors introduce Outcome-Mass Alignment to control length-dependent trajectory influence in multi-turn large language model agent reinforcement learning, improving endpoint success across matched evaluations.
Dawei Zhang, Yubin Huangfu and others · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
An LLM Is All You Need: Data-Efficient Adaptation to Full-Duplex, Word-Level Spoken Dialogue
The authors demonstrate that a frozen multimodal large language model can be adapted into a word-level full-duplex spoken dialogue system using minimal data and a four-stage curriculum.
Tairan Chen, Richard Yucheng He and others · cs.CL, cs.SD, eess.AS  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
 
ARXIV CS.LG
Machine Learning

New research investigates the energy and carbon costs of federated fine-tuning for large language models across different hardware configurations. The findings indicate that optimizing local training steps can significantly reduce energy consumption without compromising model accuracy.

1
Sustainable Federated Fine-Tuning with LoRA: Environmental Impact and Performance Footprint
The authors evaluated federated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) fine-tuning across various GPUs, finding that local training steps account for the vast majority of energy and emission variations while configurations with fewer steps yield substantially lower energy consumption.
Pedro Augusto Londe Ribeiro, Rodrigo Moreira and others · cs.LG, cs.NI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
 
ARXIV EESS.SY
Systems and Control

New research presents advancements in battery cycler technology for investigating lithium-ion cell aging under specific current conditions. The studies demonstrate improved control schemes for tracking combined direct current and sinusoidal components in battery management systems.

1
Improved Single-Input Space Vector for DC-Biased Sinusoidal Current Control in Battery Cyclers
The authors report an improved single-input space vector current control scheme for battery cyclers that enables the tracking of dc-biased sinusoidal currents to investigate lithium-ion cell aging, demonstrating through simulation and experiment that this approach is a viable alternative to proportional-integral-resonant control.
Manveer Aujla, Philip Lewoc and others · eess.SY, cs.SY  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
