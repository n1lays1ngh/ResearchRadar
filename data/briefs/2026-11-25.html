ARXIV CS.CL
Computation and Language

Recent research addresses core challenges in conversational artificial intelligence and large language model agents, ranging from utility-centric memory systems and dynamic cache consolidation to state abstraction, automated hardware design benchmarking, and safety evaluations concerning validation bias in cancer treatment queries. These studies collectively investigate how agents manage memory, process context, and maintain reliable performance across complex tasks.

1
Leto: Utility-Centric Agent Memory
The authors present Leto, a utility-centric memory system for large language model agents that frames memory as a prediction problem to anticipate future context needs, outperforming existing systems on personalization benchmarks such as PrefEval and PERMA.
Shuguang Chen and Salman Paracha · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
SD-Cache: Learnable Memory Consolidation with State-Guided Dynamic Caching
The authors introduce SD-Cache, a query-agnostic framework that consolidates layer-wise key-value states into a fixed-size working memory using selective anchor-based pooling and state-guided refinement, maintaining competitive performance under constrained memory budgets.
Yuanhong Chen, Yiping Ji and others · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
Learning Compact State Abstractions for LLM Agents
The authors propose learning compact agent state abstractions as deterministic recursive programs using an LLM-guided genetic algorithm, which preserves near-reference task success while reducing mean policy input significantly on ALFWorld and ScienceWorld.
Yaswanth Chittepu, Raghavendra Addanki and others · cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
AIChipBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Automated AI Accelerator Design
The authors introduce AIChipBench to evaluate large language model coding agents on automated AI accelerator design, finding that debugging feedback exposing stored state mismatches substantially improves acceptance rates and reduces agent-hours per accepted design.
Guangyuan Wang, Chengran Li and others · cs.CL, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
Validation Bias in Large Language Model Responses to Cancer Treatment Queries: A Factorial Cross-Sectional Study
The authors evaluate large language model chatbots using a factorial cross-sectional study and find that evidence-misaligned framing significantly increases unsafe response rates, whereas multi-turn conversations exhibit lower unsafe response rates than single-turn interactions.
Ramez Kouzy, Saketh Vinjamuri and others · cs.CL, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss

Machine Learning

Recent research addresses energy efficiency and sustainable machine learning through advances in edge speculative decoding, ultra-low-precision quantization for wearable health devices, and noise-resilient diffusion models for analog hardware. These studies demonstrate significant performance gains and energy reductions across diverse computational paradigms.

1
Pando: Noise-Coupled Parallel Draft Trees for Efficient Edge Speculative Decoding
The authors introduce Pando, a dynamic draft tree method for speculative decoding on edge devices that removes draft chain length caps via noise-coupled pruning, attaining a 5.3 times speedup and 4.2 times energy reduction over autoregressive decoding on a representative edge device.
Joren Dumoulin, Hyoukjun Kwon and others · cs.LG  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Is 4 Bits Enough for EEG Sleep Staging? A Subject-Level Benchmark of MXFP4, NVFP4 and FP8
The authors benchmark post-training quantization configurations down to block-scaled 4-bit formats for EEG sleep staging, finding that keeping the first and last layers in FP8 brings 4-bit formats within 0.48 percentage points of FP32 Macro-F1 without requiring retraining.
Emese Batizi and Hossein Valavi · cs.LG  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
Beyond Autoregression: Diffusion Language Model is All Analog In-Memory Computing Need
The authors investigate diffusion language models as an alternative to autoregressive models for analog in-memory computing, finding that diffusion models degrade more gracefully under persistent weight perturbations and tolerate 1.4 to 3 times larger weight noise on a hybrid on-board cluster.
Yuanuo Feng, Yubing Ye and others · cs.AR, cs.LG · cross-listed  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
 
ARXIV EESS.SY
Systems and Control

New research investigates the stochastic modeling of lunar power harvesting and battery recharging systems affected by random pointing errors. The study derives probability density functions for state-of-charge, capacity, and efficiency, providing quantitative insights into battery metrics under realistic lunar operating conditions.

1
Modeling Lunar Power Harvesting and Battery Recharging In The Presence of Random Pointing Error
Researchers established a lunar power beaming framework to derive probability density functions for state-of-charge, accumulated energy, and efficiency under random pointing errors, finding that slow-charging efficiency remains immune to such errors while utilizing a fraction of the link's potential capacity.
