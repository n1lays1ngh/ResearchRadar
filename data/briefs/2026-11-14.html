ARXIV CS.CL
Computation and Language

Recent research addresses critical limitations in conversational AI and LLM agents, focusing on memory systems, multi-turn tool use, security vulnerabilities, and workflow safety. These studies systematically evaluate how agents retain information, handle complex dialogues, resist memory poisoning, and maintain clinical accuracy.

1
BioMem: Agentic Memory for Biomedical LLM Agents
The authors investigate agentic memory across biomedical large language models and introduce Hybrid Memory Retrieval and Utilization, a training-free framework that achieves an average relative improvement of 8.6% across biomedical question-answering and conversational memory benchmarks.
Mingchen Li, Yongkang Xiao and others · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
ToolTurns: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Multi-Turn Dialogue
The authors introduce ToolTurns, a benchmark of 2,620 problems to evaluate large language model agents in multi-turn dialogues, finding that models perform worse on follow-up requests than on initial ones.
Milad Gholami, Brendan King and others · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
Peer Memory Changes Who LLM Agents Consult Without a Detected Gain in Answer Accuracy
Researchers investigate PeerMem, a memory mechanism for multi-agent LLM systems, finding that warm memory lowers pooled hindsight regret and increases peer consultation on specific models without a detected gain in final answer accuracy.
Jeffrey Chu, Anjali Joseph and others · cs.MA, cs.CL · cross-listed  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
MemoryDojo: Benchmarking Poisoning Through LLM Agent Memory
The authors introduce MemoryDojo, a benchmark for evaluating poisoning across five agent memory systems, and find that all tested architectures are susceptible to malicious instructions propagated through multi-session tasks.
David H. Yang, Blake Bullwinkel and others · cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.CL · cross-listed  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
Safeguarding Agentic AI in Radiology Workflows: A Multimodel Evaluation of Rule-based and Large Language Model-based Verification
A simulation study evaluating safeguards in radiology workflows reports that applying rules and a locally run LLM checker reduces high-severity violations in tool-using LLM agent documentation compared to permission-only settings.
Semil Eminovic, Robin Schmidt and others · cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CY  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
 
ARXIV CS.LG
Machine Learning

Recent research addresses the growing environmental impact of machine learning by exploring carbon-aware client selection in federated learning, joint optimization for edge inference, and the carbon footprints of federated fine-tuning and agentic AI systems. New methods also improve energy attribution for large language model batches.

1
Towards Sustainable Federated Learning via Carbon-Aware Client Selection
The authors propose a carbon-aware fair client selection strategy for federated learning to minimize client-side emissions while maintaining data diversity and global model performance.
Wentao Gao, Omid Tavallaie and others · cs.DC  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Jointly Optimizing Memory, Computing and Offloading for Energy-Efficient DNN Inference
The authors propose a joint optimization scheme integrating memory, computing, and offloading for energy-efficient deep neural network inference, demonstrating through simulation that the algorithm achieves near-optimal performance with a maximum gap of 9.3% and reduces device energy consumption by up to 16.1% compared to benchmarks.
Yunchu Han, Zhaojun Nan and others · cs.LG, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
Sustainable Federated Fine-Tuning with LoRA: Environmental Impact and Performance Footprint
The authors evaluated federated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) fine-tuning across various GPUs, finding that local training steps account for the vast majority of energy and emission variations while configurations with fewer steps yield substantially lower energy consumption.
Pedro Augusto Londe Ribeiro, Rodrigo Moreira and others · cs.LG, cs.NI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
Toward Sustainable Agentic AI: Carbon Tradeoffs Across Iterative Self-Refinement and Tool-Augmented Coding
The authors evaluate the energy and carbon footprint of agentic language model systems through empirical studies on iterative self-refinement and tool-augmented coding, finding that while refinements increase carbon costs, certain models achieve high task success and varied resolved-task-normalized efficiencies.
Boris Ruf, Adrien de Mailly Nesle and others · cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
Scaling Shapley Energy Attribution to Large LLM Batches
The authors propose StepSplit, a method using a closed-form Shapley value to efficiently attribute energy consumption to individual requests in large language model batches based on shared and request-specific inference step energies.
Qi Luo, Ziwen Wang and others · cs.DC, cs.AR, cs.PF  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
 
ARXIV EESS.SY
Systems and Control

Recent research addresses critical challenges in battery management systems by optimizing degradation-aware frequency regulation strategies and improving state-parameter estimation methods. Furthermore, new developments enhance current control techniques in battery cyclers to investigate the effects of ripple currents on cell aging.

1
Degradation-Aware Participation of Battery Storage in Frequency Regulation Markets
Researchers decompose the degradation-aware frequency regulation problem into bidding and operations layers, finding that adaptive bidding and sacrificing performance scores to avoid extreme states of charge can lengthen battery life by up to 31 percent with negligible revenue loss.
Anand Gokhale and Siddharth Bhela · eess.SY, cs.SY, math.OC  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Robust State-Parameter Estimation in Second-Order Th\'evenin Batteries
The authors propose a scheme for estimating the full states and parameters of a second-order Thévenin battery model using measured current features, finding that the method achieves exact identification, rapid series resistance recovery, and exponentially convergent online estimates under verifiable excitation conditions.
Wissam Khennoune, G. Q. Bao Tran and others · eess.SY, cs.SY, math.OC  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
Improved Single-Input Space Vector for DC-Biased Sinusoidal Current Control in Battery Cyclers
The authors report an improved single-input space vector current control scheme for battery cyclers that enables the tracking of dc-biased sinusoidal currents to investigate lithium-ion cell aging, demonstrating through simulation and experiment that this approach is a viable alternative to proportional-integral-resonant control.
Manveer Aujla, Philip Lewoc and others · eess.SY, cs.SY  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
