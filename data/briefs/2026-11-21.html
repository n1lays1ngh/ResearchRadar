Computation and Language

Recent research addresses core challenges in conversational artificial intelligence, examining long-horizon memory mechanisms, automated retrieval-augmented generation development, small language models for fast dialogue systems, and safety risks in economic environments. These studies provide empirical evaluations of autonomous agent capabilities and memory limitations across diverse operational contexts.

1
Forget Freely, Grep Later: Lossless, Index-Free Memory for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
The authors introduce GrepMem, a lossless and index-free memory mechanism combining a plain-text log with regular-expression search, which achieves 95-97 percent accuracy on ContextBench and outperforms lossy baselines without requiring complex structured memory infrastructure.
Ziyan Yang, Taiwei Shi and others · cs.CL, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Can an LLM dream? Testing the overfitted brain hypothesis on an agent with text memory
Researchers tested the overfitted brain hypothesis on a Large Language Model agent with text memory, finding that transcript corruption did not improve transfer learning and that replaced details were erroneously stored as facts.
Eric Zou · cs.CL, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
AutoRAGBench: A Benchmark for LLM Agents Automating RAG System Development
The authors propose AutoRAGBench, a new benchmark for evaluating autonomous retrieval-augmented generation system development, finding that GPT-6.1 Sol with Codex CLI achieves the highest average score and successfully improves upon human-designed systems.
Hang Zhang, Yuzhi Pan and others · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.IR  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss

Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB: An Open and Efficient Small Language Model for Fast Conversational Agents
We introduce Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB, an open and efficient small language model further post-trained from Gemma-4-E4B-it and optimized for speed and intelligence per GB of memory used for fast conversational agents.

ARXIV CS.LG
Machine Learning

Recent research addresses energy efficiency across machine learning and embedded systems by introducing novel hardware-software co-designs, specialized accelerators, and generative frameworks. These studies demonstrate significant performance speedups and energy reductions, contributing to more sustainable computational and urban infrastructures.

1
ERGo: Energy-Efficient Hybrid Graph Neural Network Training on Processing-In-Memory Architectures
The authors propose ERGo, a hybrid training framework for processing-in-memory architectures that automatically switches to a parameter-efficient phase using low-rank representations, achieving up to a 3.15x speedup and a 10.5x improvement in energy efficiency.
Pratyush Dhingra, Chibuike E. Ugwu and others · cs.LG  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Matching Architecture to Workload: Comparing RISC-V Parallel Accelerators for Energy-Efficient Embedded Computing
The authors compare three open-source RISC-V execution models—scalar, streaming, and vector processing—using a 12 nm technology implementation, finding that performance and energy efficiency vary significantly depending on workload characteristics.
Luca Colombo, Luigi Ghionda and others · cs.DC, cs.AR  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
Splice-Core: Low-Order-Bit-Splicing GEMM for Highly Energy-Efficient AI Inference Accelerators
The authors introduce Splice-Core, a temporal-unary/binary hybrid GEMM unit that reduces tile energy by 77.4% and 76.4% in Splice-BP and Splice-LUT variants respectively, while achieving substantial improvements in throughput and energy-delay product across evaluated configurations.
Prabhu Vellaisamy, Hsien-Fu Hung and others · cs.AR, cs.AI · cross-listed  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
DySENSE: Dynamic Spatiotemporal ENergy Synthesis and Emulation with Satellite Imagery
The authors introduce DySENSE, a joint multimodal generative AI framework that decouples long-term static urban morphology from short-term dynamic energy consumption to synthesize high-fidelity synthetic energy data using less than 25% labelled data.
Kailai Sun, Zijie Gao and others · cs.CV, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods
The authors explore various residential energy management methods and introduce TreeC, a machine learning-based approach that delivers competitive and transparent performance across diverse energy management problems compared to model predictive control and reinforcement learning.
Julian Ruddick · eess.SY, cs.LG, cs.NE  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss

MARS: Feature-guided physics-informed deep learning for real-time battery health and lifetime prediction
Researchers propose a multi-scale adaptive robust state-estimation framework called MARS that couples a dual-branch temporal encoder with a differentiable generalized-correntropy adaptive Kalman filter, achieving mean absolute error values of 0.72% on NASA and 0.63% on CALCE benchmarks for state-of-health estimation.
Innocent Izabayo and Haiquan Zhao · eess.SY, cs.SY  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2

Cryogenic atom probe tomography (cryo-APT) is increasingly being used to map the three-dimensional distribution of highly reactive species in heterogeneous materials, including those found in battery systems.

3
Battery Energy Storage Sizing in PV Ramp-Rate Smoothing using Constraint-Aware Reinforcement Learning-Based Controller
