ARXIV CS.CL
Computation and Language

Recent research addresses critical challenges across conversational AI, dialogue systems, and large language model agents, examining memory scaling, automated retrieval-augmented generation development, parasocial behavior reinforcement, financial verification architectures, and bilingual pragmatic inference. These studies highlight ongoing advancements and persistent limitations in multi-turn reliability, evaluation methodologies, and cross-linguistic deployment.

1
Forget Freely, Grep Later: Lossless, Index-Free Memory for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
The authors introduce GrepMem, a lossless and index-free memory mechanism combining a plain-text log with regular-expression search, which achieves 95-97 percent accuracy on ContextBench and outperforms lossy baselines without requiring complex structured memory infrastructure.
Ziyan Yang, Taiwei Shi and others · cs.CL, cs.AI  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
AutoRAGBench: A Benchmark for LLM Agents Automating RAG System Development
The authors propose AutoRAGBench, a new benchmark for evaluating autonomous retrieval-augmented generation system development, finding that GPT-6.1 Sol with Codex CLI achieves the highest average score and successfully improves upon human-designed systems.
Hang Zhang, Yuzhi Pan and others · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.IR  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
CoMPASS: A Multi-Turn Benchmark for Measuring LLM Reinforcement of Parasocial Behavior
The authors introduce CoMPASS, a multi-turn benchmark measuring how conversational large language models reinforce parasocial attachment, observing that multi-turn escalation increases parasocial behavior across evaluated models and that provider-level choices significantly influence these outcomes.
Shwetanshu Singh, Mark Keavney and others · cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.HC  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
FinVeriQA: Financial Research Agents for Question Answering: Routing, Retrieval, MCP Tool Use, and Provenance-Aware Evidence Verification
The authors present FinVeriQA, an open-source multi-agent financial research prototype, demonstrating that routing models, programmatic calculations, and code validation address common large language model failure modes in financial question answering despite remaining vulnerabilities in shared evidence verification.
Meenakshi Rajpurohit, Anna Phan and others · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.IR  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
PILAPS: A Bilingual Benchmark for Evaluating Pragmatic Inference across Phenomena and Settings
The authors introduce PILAPS, a bilingual English and Mandarin Chinese benchmark for evaluating pragmatic inference, and find substantial variation across models and phenomena, with open-source systems struggling particularly with humor and models showing a higher propensity for literal interpretation than humans.
Jiani Cao, Anna Korhonen and others · cs.CL  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
 
ARXIV CS.LG
Machine Learning

Recent research addresses energy efficiency and sustainability in computing by evaluating RISC-V accelerators, introducing low-order-bit-splicing GEMM units for AI inference, and exploring residential energy management methods. Additional studies examine black-box attacks on vision-language models and conditional diffusion frameworks for few-shot traffic prediction.

1
Matching Architecture to Workload: Comparing RISC-V Parallel Accelerators for Energy-Efficient Embedded Computing
The authors compare three open-source RISC-V execution models—scalar, streaming, and vector processing—using a 12 nm technology implementation, finding that performance and energy efficiency vary significantly depending on workload characteristics.
Luca Colombo, Luigi Ghionda and others · cs.DC, cs.AR  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
2
Splice-Core: Low-Order-Bit-Splicing GEMM for Highly Energy-Efficient AI Inference Accelerators
The authors introduce Splice-Core, a temporal-unary/binary hybrid GEMM unit that reduces tile energy by 77.4% and 76.4% in Splice-BP and Splice-LUT variants respectively, while achieving substantial improvements in throughput and energy-delay product across evaluated configurations.
Prabhu Vellaisamy, Hsien-Fu Hung and others · cs.AR, cs.AI · cross-listed  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
3
How to Effectively Manage the Energy in Residential Systems: Development and Comparison of Methods
The authors explore various residential energy management methods and introduce TreeC, a machine learning-based approach that delivers competitive and transparent performance across diverse energy management problems compared to model predictive control and reinforcement learning.
Julian Ruddick · eess.SY, cs.LG, cs.NE  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
4
ChronoPatch: Black-Box Energy-Latency Attacks on Vision-Language Models
The authors present ChronoPatch, a black-box attack that utilizes final-response-only observation to induce prolonged vision-language model generation, increasing inference latency by 47.86% to 293.34% and GPU energy consumption by 41.44% to 249.70% across four open-source models.
Jiawei Xing, Li Wang and others · cs.CR, cs.AI · cross-listed  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
5
From Data Alignment to Parameter Generation: Few-Shot Cross-City Traffic Prediction with Conditional Diffusion
The authors propose a Conditional Diffusion-based Parameter Generation framework for few-shot cross-city traffic prediction, which generates node-specific model parameters conditioned on multi-modal spatio-temporal context rather than aligning raw data.
Shengchun Wang, Yuhang Lin and others · cs.LG  ·  Abstract  ·  PDF  ·  Discuss
